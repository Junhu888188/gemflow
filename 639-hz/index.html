639 Hz: the heart chakra frequency, and what we actually know

639 Hz is a clear tone that vibrates 639 times a second, between two notes on the piano. It is one of the six original Solfeggio frequencies, paired with the heart chakra and labelled “connecting” or “relationships”. The label comes from a 1999 book, and no study has tested the tone itself. Below: the history, the meaning, safety, and how to listen.

The basics

What is 639 Hz?

639 Hz means 639 vibrations per second. In standard tuning (A above middle C = 440 Hz) it falls almost exactly between two notes: E♭5 at 622 Hz and E5 at 659 Hz, about 46 cents (just under half a semitone) above the E♭. So it does not match any note on a piano. It is the fourth of the six original Solfeggio frequencies, and most lists pair it with the heart chakra.

The history

Where did 639 Hz come from?

It is one of the six numbers made famous by Leonard Horowitz’s book Healing Codes for the Biological Apocalypse (1999). According to Horowitz, the herbalist Joseph Puleo found them in the verse numbers of the Book of Numbers, chapter 7, and Horowitz matched them to the syllables of the medieval hymn Ut queant laxis. 639 Hz became “Fa”. Guido of Arezzo’s syllables named the steps of a scale, not fixed pitches, so the number itself is modern.

The label

What does 639 Hz mean?

Horowitz’s list gives 639 Hz the label “connecting” or “relationships”, and today it is paired with the heart chakra, the one traditionally tied to love and connection. Both are traditions, not research findings. Many people use it as a quiet moment to think of someone they care about, which is a perfectly good reason to listen.

The research

Does 639 Hz do anything?

We found no peer-reviewed study that tested 639 Hz on its own. The only Solfeggio tone with even a small human study is 528 Hz: nine adults had lower cortisol after five minutes of piano music tuned to it. Calm music and slow breathing help many people relax, and 639 Hz can be part of that. There is no evidence that the tone itself improves relationships or “opens” the heart.

Source: Akimoto K et al., Effect of 528 Hz Music on the Endocrine System and Autonomic Nervous System, Health 10(9), 2018

Safety

Is 639 Hz safe?

Yes, at a comfortable volume. It is an ordinary audible tone in the middle of the range of a singing voice. The only real risk is loudness: the WHO and ITU safe-listening standard suggests keeping personal audio around 80 decibels or lower, for no more than 40 hours a week.

Source: WHO–ITU, Global standard for safe listening devices and systems (H.870, 2019)

How to listen

What does 639 Hz sound like?

A clear, middle-high tone, a little above E♭5 on the piano. Because it falls between two piano notes, it can sound slightly “off” against tuned music, so many tracks pair it with singing bowls, drones or nature sounds instead. Keep it soft, place a hand on your chest if you like, and breathe slowly for ten minutes.
